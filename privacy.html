Privacy Policy

Wynne for iPhone and Android · Last updated: 26 September 2026

In short: Wynne keeps your check-ins, plans and history on your phone. There is no account, no tracking, no advertising and no analytics SDK in the app, and we never analyse your check-ins or your sessions. Data leaves your phone in four cases only: for Wynne Plus (Apple or Google, and RevenueCat, which also gives us subscription figures), when you enter a reviewer access code (our server at Supabase), when the app looks for new sessions at that same server, and when it loads an optional text update from this website. None of those four carries a name, an account or anything you entered — and nothing you type in the app is ever sent anywhere.

Data that stays on your device

Everything you enter in Wynne is stored only in the app's local storage on your phone and is never sent to us:

  • your setup answers (for example what shapes your day, your usual time window, your experience with Pilates, areas to go easy on, and the optional period-day and midlife settings),
  • your daily check-ins (time, place, energy, body areas, optional period day),
  • your day plans, finished or paused sessions and your one-tap feedback after a session,
  • your settings, such as reminder time, voice and music,

Some of these answers relate to your health (for example tender body areas or period days). They are processed only on your phone, so we never receive them. Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your phone; Wynne asks for permission only after you turn a reminder on. You can delete all of this data in the app under Settings → Reset my data, or by uninstalling the app.

Purchases (Apple, Google and RevenueCat)

When this happens. Not only when you buy something. RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., USA) starts together with the app: at every app start Wynne asks it, under an anonymous and randomly generated app user ID, whether Wynne Plus is currently active — before and without any purchase, because the app has to know which sessions to unlock. If you then start a trial or subscribe, Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) processes the payment under your store account; we never see your payment details.

What RevenueCat receives. The anonymous app user ID, your purchase and subscription status (transaction data from the store), and technical data such as app version, device model, operating system, country/storefront and IP address. It is not linked to your name or your email address — we hold neither.

What it is used for. Three purposes: to unlock Wynne Plus, to restore it (for example on a new phone), and to analyse subscriptions — RevenueCat's dashboard shows us figures derived from this data, such as active subscriptions, trial conversions, cancellations and revenue. That analysis is about subscriptions, not about you as a person, and it never covers your check-ins, plans or sessions: those stay on your phone.

See RevenueCat's privacy policy, Apple's privacy policy and Google's privacy policy.

Legal basis (GDPR): performance of the contract you enter into when you subscribe (Art. 6(1)(b)); for the status check before a purchase and for the subscription figures, our legitimate interest in providing the paid tier correctly and in understanding how it is used (Art. 6(1)(f)). RevenueCat processes data in the USA; the transfer is based on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and/or Standard Contractual Clauses.

Reviewer access codes (Supabase)

Under Settings → Reviewer access, app reviewers and competition judges can enter an access code that turns on Wynne Plus without a purchase. This is one of the two parts of Wynne that talk to a server of ours — the other is the chat, described below: our database at Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), hosted in Frankfurt, Germany (region eu-central-1).

When this happens. In two situations only: when you enter a code, and — once a code has been accepted — at every app start after that, to check whether the access still stands. That re-check is the point of the server: it is how an access code that was withdrawn actually stops working on the device. In both cases the app first signs in anonymously: Supabase issues a random user ID and certifies it, which is what lets the database show each identity only its own rows. There is still no account, no user name, no password, no email address.

What is sent and stored. Sent: the code you typed, together with that anonymous identity. Stored on the server: the anonymous user ID with the times it was created and last used; which code was redeemed by that ID and when; and one row per attempt with the time and whether it succeeded — this is what limits guessing to ten failed attempts per hour. The codes themselves exist only as a SHA-256 hash, so the database cannot hand out a working code. Your name, your email address and your check-ins, plans and session history are never sent: the app has no account, and your history stays on the phone.

If you never enter a code, the app never signs in there: no account, no identity, nothing about you. It does still ask that same provider for new sessions — anonymously, described under „Content updates“ below. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR (providing the access, and protecting the codes against guessing). See Supabase's privacy policy.

Content updates

New sessions. Once per cold start the app asks our server at Supabase (Frankfurt, Germany) whether there are sessions beyond the ones built into it. It is a plain read: one request, carrying only the app’s own public project key — no account, no sign-in, no identifier of yours, and nothing you entered. Like any web request it reveals your IP address to the host for delivery. Nothing is written on the server, and the app is complete without the answer: every session you can start is already in the app when it launches. See Supabase’s privacy policy.

When the app starts, it may also download one small text file with session text corrections from this website (GitHub Pages, operated by GitHub, Inc., USA). Like any web request, this reveals your IP address to the host for delivery; the app sends no identifier of its own, only the cache tag of the file it already has (If-None-Match), so an unchanged file is not downloaded twice. The app works fully without it. See GitHub's privacy statement.

Our processors

RevenueCat and Supabase process this data for us and on our instructions, for the purposes named above and no others. All three are bound by a data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR, which obliges them to confidentiality and to appropriate technical and organisational security — see RevenueCat's DPA and Supabase's DPA. We do not sell data and we pass it to no one else, except where we are legally required to.

How long we keep data

  • On your phone: until you delete it under Settings → Reset my data or uninstall the app. We set no period here, because this data never reaches us.
  • Purchase data at RevenueCat: for as long as a Wynne Plus purchase can still be restored under that anonymous app user ID — that is what the record is for — and for as long as we need the subscription figures described above. On request we have it deleted within 30 days (see Delete data). RevenueCat additionally applies its own retention periods; see its privacy policy linked above.
  • Reviewer access data at Supabase: for as long as the access code it belongs to is in use. The two codes that exist today expire on 30 November 2026 and 31 March 2027; when a code is retired we delete its redemption and attempt records, and earlier on request. An attempt record has an effect for one hour — that is the window the guessing limit counts in.
  • Server logs: requests to Supabase and to GitHub Pages leave the usual connection data, including your IP address, in those providers' logs. They are kept and deleted under those providers' own policies, which are linked above; we do not build user profiles from them.

When one of these purposes ends, the data is deleted — unless a statutory retention duty (for example under tax or commercial law) still applies to it.

What we do not do

  • No account, no login, no email address.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, no advertising identifiers, no ads.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK in the app, and no analysis of your check-ins, plans or sessions. What we do analyse are subscription figures at RevenueCat — see Purchases above.
  • No model writes what Wynne says, and no model ever sees your check-ins, your plans or your history — only the one sentence you type in the chat, and only to be turned into settings the app already has.
  • No selling of data.
  • No access to Apple Health, Health Connect, your location, camera, microphone or contacts.

Children

Wynne is made for adults. It is not directed at children under 16 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Details: Delete data.

Prüf-Zugangscodes (Supabase)

Unter Einstellungen → Prüfzugang können App-Prüferinnen und Wettbewerbsjurys einen Zugangscode eingeben, der Wynne Plus ohne Kauf einschaltet. Das ist einer der beiden Teile von Wynne, die mit einem Server von uns sprechen — der andere ist das Gespräch, weiter unten: unserer Datenbank bei Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), betrieben in Frankfurt am Main (Region eu-central-1).

Wann das passiert. Nur in zwei Fällen: wenn du einen Code eingibst und — nachdem ein Code angenommen wurde — bei jedem weiteren App-Start, um zu prüfen, ob der Zugang noch gilt. Genau dafür gibt es den Server: So verliert ein zurückgezogener Zugangscode auf dem Gerät tatsächlich seine Wirkung. In beiden Fällen meldet sich die App zuerst anonym an: Supabase stellt eine zufällige Nutzer-ID aus und beglaubigt sie; erst dadurch kann die Datenbank jeder Identität nur ihre eigenen Zeilen zeigen. Es bleibt bei: kein Konto, kein Nutzername, kein Passwort, keine E-Mail-Adresse.

Was gesendet und gespeichert wird. Gesendet wird der eingegebene Code zusammen mit dieser anonymen Kennung. Auf dem Server gespeichert werden: die anonyme Nutzer-ID mit dem Zeitpunkt ihrer Erstellung und ihrer letzten Nutzung; welcher Code von dieser Kennung wann eingelöst wurde; und je Versuch eine Zeile mit Zeitpunkt und der Angabe, ob er erfolgreich war — damit wird das Raten auf zehn Fehlversuche pro Stunde begrenzt. Die Codes selbst liegen nur als SHA-256-Hash vor; die Datenbank kann also keinen gültigen Code herausgeben. Dein Name, deine E-Mail-Adresse sowie deine Check-ins, Pläne und dein Einheiten-Verlauf werden nie gesendet: Die App hat kein Konto, und dein Verlauf bleibt auf dem Telefon.

Gibst du nie einen Code ein, meldet sich die App dort nie an: kein Konto, keine Identität, nichts über dich. Sie fragt denselben Anbieter aber weiterhin nach neuen Einheiten — anonym, beschrieben unter „Inhalts-Updates“. Rechtsgrundlage: Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. b und f DSGVO (Zugang bereitstellen, Codes vor Erraten schützen). Siehe Datenschutzerklärung von Supabase.
